# CECS 378 Reading Assignment: Social Engineering

### Assignment Description
Answer the following questions from our in-class discussion. Be complete with your answers. You may work on these questions with one or two other partners, but *all* students must submit the document individually in their own repositories along with each student's name documented with the submission.

1. Robert Cialdini identified six core principles of influence that underlie most social engineering attacks. List and briefly define each principle, and for each one, provide a concrete example of a social engineering attack that relies *primarily* on that principle.

2. Compare and contrast *phishing*, *spearphishing*, and *whaling*. What distinguishes each attack type in terms of targeting strategy, effort required by the attacker, and typical payload or goal? Which do you believe poses the greatest risk to a university environment, and why?

3. Consider the following email message:

    > **Subject: URGENT: Your university account will be suspended**
    >
    > Your university account will be suspended in 2 hours due to suspicious login activity. Click the link below immediately to verify your credentials and restore access.
    >
    > — IT Security Team

    1. Identify every social engineering technique present in this message, citing which of Cialdini's principles each technique exploits.

    2. Rewrite the message to incorporate at least two *additional* Cialdini principles not present in the original, making it more convincing. Briefly explain each addition.

4. A company wants to assess employee susceptibility to USB baiting attacks. Design a brief test plan addressing the following:

    1. What would the USB drives contain to allow measurement of results (no actual malicious code required — describe the mechanism conceptually)?

    2. Where and how would you distribute the drives?

    3. How would you measure and record results?

    4. How should employees who plug in the drives be handled afterward, and should employees be informed in advance that such tests may occur? Justify your answer.

5. The 2011 RSA SecurID breach began with a single employee opening a phishing email with an attachment titled *"2011 Recruitment Plan.xls"*.

    1. Trace the full attack chain from that initial click through to the eventual impact on U.S. defense contractor networks.

    2. Identify at least **four** distinct points in the chain where the attack could have been stopped, and name the specific security control required at each point.

6. Verizon's *Data Breach Investigations Report* (DBIR) consistently finds that over 80% of breaches involve a human element. Some security researchers argue this means organizations should redesign systems to *assume human failure as a baseline condition* rather than trying to train it away.

    1. Construct the strongest argument *in favor* of the "design for human failure" position.

    2. Construct the strongest argument *against* it.

    3. State and defend your own position.

7. Using only publicly available OSINT sources (e.g., LinkedIn, a university department page, a company website), describe the steps an attacker would take to build a pretext for a spearphishing attack against a fictional mid-level employee at a technology company.

    1. What categories of information would be gathered, and from which sources?

    2. How would that information be used to make a phishing message credible?

    3. What specific details would make a target *more* or *less* vulnerable based on their public footprint?

    > **Note:** Do not target any real individual. Use a fictional persona for this exercise.

8. Compare the *2020 Twitter Bitcoin Hack* and the *2015 Ubiquiti BEC Scam*. Both caused significant damage but used different attack vectors targeting different organizational functions.

    1. What do these two incidents share at the *root cause* level?

    2. What single class of organizational control would have meaningfully reduced risk in *both* cases? Justify your answer.

9. Many organizations deploy annual security awareness training — typically a one-hour video module followed by a short quiz — as their primary human-layer defense against social engineering.

    1. Drawing on how social engineering attacks exploit human cognition, explain why this approach is likely insufficient.

    2. Propose a more effective alternative security awareness program. For each element you include, provide a specific justification grounded in the mechanisms of social engineering.

10. A penetration testing firm is hired by a hospital to conduct a full social engineering assessment. During the engagement, a tester successfully convinces a nurse to reveal a patient's room number and current medication schedule by posing as a concerned family member over the phone. The information is never misused, and the nurse is debriefed afterward and informed it was a test.

    1. Was this test ethical? Justify your position.

    2. Identify all parties who bear some degree of responsibility for the outcome — the tester, the firm, and the hospital — and characterize each party's responsibility.

    3. Does it matter whether the nurse was previously informed that such tests might occur? How does prior disclosure affect your ethical analysis?

    > **Note:** There is no single correct answer to this question. You will be evaluated on the quality of your reasoning and how well you engage with competing considerations, not on which conclusion you reach.
